

## cycling training TSS increase per week relative to CTL 35

*Searched: 2026-05-29 00:42 UTC*

When planning your training progression, it is important to distinguish between **CTL (Chronic Training Load)**, which measures your long-term fitness trend, and **weekly TSS (Training Stress Score)**, which is the immediate load you accumulate.

A CTL of 35 is generally considered a starting or "base" level for many riders. Because your current fitness level is relatively low, your body may be more susceptible to fatigue or injury from rapid increases in training volume or intensity.

### 1. The "Ramp Rate" (CTL Increase)
Coaches and training platforms generally focus on the **CTL ramp rate**—how much your 42-day rolling average of daily TSS increases per week. 
*   **Recommended Ramp Rate:** A common, conservative guideline for most athletes is to increase CTL by **3–5 points per week** during a build phase.
*   **The Upper Limit:** Increasing CTL by more than **5–8 points per week** is often considered aggressive and carries a higher risk of overtraining, injury, or burnout, especially for those at lower fitness levels.

### 2. Weekly TSS Increase
While CTL is a smoothed metric, your **weekly TSS** is the actual work you perform. You should not attempt to increase your total weekly TSS by more than **10% per week**.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you are currently averaging 300 TSS per week, a safe increase would be approximately 30 TSS. 
*   **Consistency over Spikes:** Large jumps in TSS (e.g., adding 100+ points in a single week) can cause your Acute Training Load (ATL) to spike, which increases fatigue and can lead to a significant drop in your Training Stress Balance (TSB), indicating you are overreaching.

### 3. Practical Recommendations for Your Level
*   **The 3:1 Cycle:** A widely accepted method is the "3 weeks on, 1 week off" structure. Increase your training load (volume or intensity) for three weeks, then intentionally reduce your TSS by 30–50% during the fourth week to allow for recovery and supercompensation.
*   **Listen to Subjective Metrics:** At a CTL of 35, prioritize how you feel over the raw numbers. If your TSB (Form) drops deeply into the negative, or if you feel lethargic, unmotivated, or have a high resting heart rate, you have reached your immediate limit.
*   **Focus on Consistency:** A steady increase of 5% per week sustained over two months will yield better physiological adaptations than a "yo-yo" approach of large spikes followed by forced recovery due to fatigue.

### Summary Checklist
*   **Aim for a CTL ramp rate of 3–5 points/week.**
*   **Limit total weekly TSS increases to ~10%.**
*   **Use a 3:1 load-to-recovery ratio.**
*   **Adjust based on "sample of one":** If you are consistently tired, reduce the ramp rate.
